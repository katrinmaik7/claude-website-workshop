# Express offer (offer + positioning)

Read `CLAUDE.md`, the active session files through `02-research.md`, `.claude/references/SESSION-AND-EVIDENCE.md`, `.claude/references/MARKET-RESEARCH.md`, and `.claude/references/CONVERSION-STRATEGY.md`.

## Entry gate

Run the mandatory progression gate in `CLAUDE.md`. Require `Path: Express`, `Next command: /express-offer`, and complete approved `00-setup-and-vision.md` and `02-research.md`. Otherwise stop and name the earliest incomplete command.

## Gap questions

Ask at most two questions, one per turn, and only for material gaps not already answered:

- What exactly does the customer receive, how will it be sourced or delivered, and what price, material costs, time, or operational dependencies are known?
- What proof and delivery capability exist today: work you can show, relevant customer behavior or feedback, credentials, suppliers, capacity, or results with context?

Recommend everything else from the evidence — who it is not for, what happens after the action — and mark it `Hypothesis` if unconfirmed. Never invent pricing, guarantees, scarcity, outcomes, credentials, or proof.

## Develop

Build the offer statement from `CONVERSION-STRATEGY.md`: audience and situation, outcome, mechanism and deliverables, commitment, proof and limits, fit boundary, primary website action.

Run the compact desirability, feasibility, and viability gate from `CONVERSION-STRATEGY.md`. Compare the proposed price with the observed market range and record whether it is evidence-based, an experiment price, or unknown. Record material cost drivers, fulfillment method, capacity, dependencies, and rough contribution or explicit unknown. Participant approval does not convert an unsupported price or demand assumption into evidence.

Assign the result one status: `Evidence-backed offer`, `Test offer`, or `Demo only`. If direct customer or commercial evidence is weak, frame the downstream website as a measured test and define what must be captured. If the action cannot capture evidence, use `Demo only`.

Then build the positioning chain: genuine alternatives in the priority situation → what differs → the value that creates → who cares most → market frame → proof available and proof still needed. Output a plain-language positioning decision, not a slogan.

Offer a second option only when the evidence supports a real choice, and say which one you recommend and why.

## Checkpoint

Show at most five bullets: the offer and its status, commitment and price status, alternative and meaningful difference, reason to believe and its limit, and the largest desirability, feasibility, or viability risk plus the planned test. Ask one confirmation or correction question.

## Save

After confirmation, write only `04-offer-and-positioning.md` with:

1. offer statement with each element labelled;
2. positioning chain and decision;
3. competitive frame and differentiated value;
4. proof available, proof required, and prohibited claims;
5. desirability-feasibility-viability table, price basis, cost drivers, dependencies, and offer status;
6. fit boundary and rejected alternatives with reasons;
7. validation mechanism, risks, falsifying evidence, and open questions for `/brand-concept`.

Update `SESSION.md`. Finish with the outcome, saved file, and “Next: `/brand-concept`”.
