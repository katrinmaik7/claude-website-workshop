# Express research scan (market + audience + commercial reality)

Read `CLAUDE.md`, the active session's `SESSION.md` and `00-setup-and-vision.md`, `.claude/references/SESSION-AND-EVIDENCE.md`, `.claude/references/MARKET-RESEARCH.md`, and `.claude/references/AUDIENCE-RESEARCH.md`.

## Entry gate

Run the mandatory progression gate in `CLAUDE.md`. Require `Path: Express`, `Next command: /express-research`, and a complete approved `00-setup-and-vision.md`. Otherwise stop and direct the participant to `/express-start`.

## Research frame

State the research question, market, geography, starting audience hypothesis, and the website decision this research must support. Ask a scope question only if geography or language is genuinely unresolved.

Build a short search plan before browsing, without displaying internal planning. Cover:

- category and direct offers;
- indirect alternatives and the status quo;
- prices, inclusions, business models, fulfillment, and trust mechanisms;
- audience situations, current behavior, desired progress, objections, and language;
- demand signals and disconfirming evidence.

## Five-minute research pass

Use a hard research timebox of about five minutes. Browse directly in the current task and batch independent searches when the available tools support it. Do not launch a separate long-running, delegated, or background research task. Do not announce a longer estimate or wait for exhaustive coverage.

Open every source used. Aim for 6–8 useful sources with different evidence roles:

1. 2–3 relevant direct alternatives, prioritizing official offer and pricing pages;
2. 1–2 indirect or status-quo alternatives when they materially affect the decision;
3. 2 independent sources of customer language, such as contextual reviews, forum threads, Q&A, community discussions, or comparable public material;
4. optionally one credible category, behavioral, public-data, trade, or primary-research source when it can resolve a material question quickly;
5. at least one meaningful piece of evidence that contradicts, narrows, or complicates the founder's starting view.

These ranges are decision-coverage targets, not mechanical quotas. Stop when the timebox is reached or eight useful sources have been opened, whichever comes first. Stop earlier when additional sources repeat the same evidence. If access is slow or a source type cannot be found, continue with the strongest available set and record the limitation instead of extending the task.

Prioritize relevant participant-provided interviews, customer messages, sales notes, or behavioral evidence over collecting more public pages. The result is a directional workshop scan, not validation and not a comprehensive market study.

Reuse a source when it genuinely supports more than one question. Search-result snippets, AI summaries, listicles, and unsourced market-size claims are discovery leads rather than evidence. Open the original page. Do not estimate market size or willingness to pay from weak proxies.

For customer-language sources, record who appears to be speaking, the situation, whether they are a buyer or user when knowable, and the limitation. Do not combine isolated comments into a claim of prevalence.

## Analysis

Cover:

- market and alternative map: direct, indirect, and status quo;
- compact competitor comparison: audience or situation, promise, offer, price when visible, proof or trust mechanism, friction, and message pattern;
- category patterns: crowded promises, expected features, trust conventions, and meaningful gaps found among the reviewed alternatives;
- audience situations: triggers, current behaviour, desired progress, decision criteria, anxieties, objections, and switching barriers;
- functional, emotional, and social progress where evidence supports each layer;
- a customer-language bank with source, speaker context, situation, and safe use;
- demand signals: existing paid behaviour, repeat-use patterns, active workaround effort, search or discussion behaviour when credible, and other observable commitment signals;
- counter-evidence: low urgency, weak frequency, substitute satisfaction, price resistance, operational difficulty, or evidence against the selected audience;
- price bands and what is included, plus delivery, capacity, supplier, timing, geography, quality, and cost signals;
- 2 plausible audience situations or segments, compared before selecting one test audience;
- differentiation ideas that pass the relevant / distinct / provable / deliverable test, and those that fail;
- an evidence-strength table with `Safe to claim`, `Safe to test`, `Unknown`, and `What would change the decision`;
- 3 implications for the offer, positioning, website message, proof, and conversion experiment.
- a compact website evidence pack using the outcome-contract IDs in `SESSION-AND-EVIDENCE.md`: evidence for `D1`–`D3`, candidate `D5`, proof and trust inputs for `D7`, and objections or trade-offs for `D8`. For each, include the customer-facing consequence and the exact source or evidence gap.

Label everything. Public comments are directional, not validation. Do not estimate market size. Keep participant-provided evidence, public-signal research, and future direct research separate; name the stronger follow-up method required for every high-risk conclusion.

Do not infer demand merely because competitors exist, people complain, a search term exists, or an idea receives engagement. Distinguish evidence of a problem, evidence of paid behavior, and evidence that the proposed solution would be chosen.

## Checkpoint

Show at most five bullets: the market pattern and alternatives that matter, the compared audience situations and selected test audience, the strongest demand and customer-language evidence, the largest contradiction or commercial risk, and the recommended website experiment. Ask one confirmation or correction question. Use “evidence-backed audience” only when the threshold in `AUDIENCE-RESEARCH.md` is met.

## Quality gate

- Sources serve distinct evidence roles and every material conclusion has traceable support or an explicit gap.
- Direct, indirect, and status-quo alternatives are represented.
- Several audience situations are compared before one is selected.
- Customer language retains speaker and situation context.
- Problem evidence, paid-behavior evidence, and solution-choice evidence are not conflated.
- Prices are compared with inclusions and fulfillment differences rather than as isolated numbers.
- At least one meaningful contradiction or failed assumption is reported, or the search limitation explains why none was found.
- Participant approval does not upgrade evidence strength.
- The report states what desk research cannot establish and specifies the next direct or behavioral validation.
- The evidence pack contains concrete downstream message, offer, proof, objection, and conversion implications rather than only market description.
