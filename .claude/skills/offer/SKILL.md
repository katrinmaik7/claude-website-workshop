# Offer

Read `CLAUDE.md`, active session files through `03-audience-research.md`, `.claude/references/SESSION-AND-EVIDENCE.md`, and `.claude/references/CONVERSION-STRATEGY.md`.

## Entry gate

Run the mandatory progression gate in `CLAUDE.md`. Require `Path: Full`, `Next command: /offer`, and complete approved stage files through `03-audience-research.md`. Otherwise stop and name the earliest incomplete command.

## Gap-first questions

Identify the smallest set of material gaps. Ask one question at a time, only as needed:

- What exactly does the customer receive or experience?
- How does the offer create the desired progress?
- What time, effort, price, or commitment is required?
- What proof exists today?
- How will the offer be sourced, produced, fulfilled, delivered, supported, and refunded when relevant?
- What are the material unit costs, capacity limits, dependencies, and price basis?
- Who is it not suitable for?
- What happens immediately after the primary action?

Do not ask questions already answered in the session.

## Develop options

Create 2–3 materially different offer formulations only when a real choice exists. For each, explain fit with evidence, delivery implications, proof needs, and risk. Recommend one without pretending certainty.

Never invent pricing, guarantees, scarcity, outcomes, credentials, or proof. Mark unresolved items clearly.

Before recommending an offer, run the desirability, feasibility, and viability gate in `CONVERSION-STRATEGY.md`. Produce a compact table covering customer evidence, delivery capability, important dependencies, price basis, competitor price range, material costs, and rough contribution or explicit unknown. A participant-approved price remains a hypothesis when its commercial basis is unknown.

Assign one status:

- `Evidence-backed offer`;
- `Test offer`;
- `Demo only`.

When feasibility or viability is materially unknown, do not describe the offer as ready to sell. Define the smallest test and the evidence that would change the decision.

For a `Test offer`, record that later stages must preserve the intended measurable action as `Connected` or `Connection required before launch`. They may change it to a non-collecting prototype only after the participant explicitly accepts which CTA, follow-up, and learning will be lost. Missing implementation is a launch blocker, not a reason to strip the public proposition of its approved offer, price, differentiation, or fit.

## Synthesis checkpoint

Propose the offer in plain language:

- audience and situation;
- outcome;
- mechanism and deliverables;
- commitment;
- proof and limitations;
- offer status, price status, and the largest feasibility or viability risk;
- fit boundary;
- website action and next step.

Ask one confirmation question.

## Save

After confirmation, write only `04-offer.md`, including the desirability-feasibility-viability table, price and cost status, offer status, rejected alternatives, the smallest validation test, and the evidence that would falsify the choice. Update `SESSION.md`.

Finish with the outcome, saved file, and “Next: `/positioning`”.
