# Brand concept method

## Purpose

The brand concept is the single approved synthesis of the workshop's business research and the founder's identity decisions. It translates earlier work into the ten Brand Concept blocks used by website structure, website copy, and Visual Direction.

It must not become a second market, audience, offer, or positioning exercise. Import approved upstream decisions and cite their source instead of asking the participant to recreate them. Ask only for identity choices that research cannot make on the founder's behalf.

The final source of truth is `06-brand-concept.md`. Earlier stage files remain the source of detailed evidence; the brand concept records the approved brand decision, a concise rationale, its status, and its source.

## Decision sources and status

Keep these sources separate:

| Source | Can establish | Cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Founder truth | Purpose, beliefs, values, history, identity boundaries and commitments | Demand or audience relevance |
| Business truth | Capabilities, delivery behavior, proof and operational trade-offs | Desired perception by itself |
| Audience evidence | Situations, needs, language, trust requirements and perception risks | Founder values or purpose |
| Market evidence | Category conventions, alternatives, crowded claims and opportunities | A distinctive identity by itself |
| Strategic interpretation | A recommendation connecting the approved evidence | A fact before approval or testing |

Give each material decision one evidence label from `SESSION-AND-EVIDENCE.md` and one source pointer. The pointer says where the decision came from; the label says how firm it is:

| Situation | Label and pointer |
|---|---|
| Approved in an earlier stage and unchanged | `Decision — <stage file> §<section>` |
| Chosen or approved by the founder in this stage | `Decision — founder, this stage` |
| Required by audience, market, offer, proof, legal, accessibility, or language evidence | `Observed` or `Inferred`, with the stage file and section |
| Synthesis proposed here from the evidence | `Inferred`, with the sources it rests on |
| Useful direction not yet supported strongly enough | `Hypothesis` |

Do not use a smooth narrative to hide where a decision came from.

## No-duplication rule

Full path:

- `02-market-research.md` owns detailed market and competitor evidence.
- `03-audience-research.md` owns detailed audience evidence and customer language.
- `04-offer.md` owns deliverables, mechanism, fit, commitment, proof and promise limits.
- `05-positioning.md` owns category, alternatives, differentiated value and competitive territory.

Express path:

- `02-research.md` owns market, competitor, and audience evidence and customer language.
- `04-offer-and-positioning.md` owns the offer, proof limits, alternatives, differentiated value and competitive territory.

On both paths, `06-brand-concept.md` owns the approved expression system derived from those decisions plus founder identity choices.

In `06-brand-concept.md`, summarize only what downstream work needs. Link each imported decision to its stage file and section. Do not copy research tables, source records, interview notes, competitor teardowns, or alternative options unless an unresolved conflict materially affects the brand decision.

## Ten-block schema

### 01. Brand Essence

Fields:

- **Core idea:** what the brand is, what it does, and for whom.
- **Purpose:** the useful change the founder chooses to pursue.
- **Promise:** the meaningful value the business can make real for people.
- **Value proposition:** the clear market benefit relative to relevant alternatives.

Import the audience, customer progress, offer value, mechanism, proof, and positioning. The founder must approve purpose and the final expression. Keep purpose, promise, and value proposition distinct. A promise cannot exceed the evidence and operating model in the approved offer file.

Reject an essence that a close competitor could use unchanged.

### 02. Positioning

Fields:

- **Category**
- **Audience**
- **Market context**
- **Differentiation**
- **Competitive territory**

Import these from the research and positioning files of the session's path. Do not reopen positioning merely to make the wording sound more branded. If the participant wants a strategic change, identify the conflict and return to a new session rather than silently rewriting the approved decision.

### 03. Brand Personality

Fields:

- **Personality traits:** three or four traits that change behavior.
- **Desired perception:** how the right customer should describe the brand after a real experience.
- **Tensions or contrasts:** a productive pair that guides choices rather than decorative adjectives.

For each trait define:

| Trait | Business behavior | Communication behavior | Anti-behavior |
|---|---|---|---|

Audience evidence may reveal trust requirements and perception risks, but it does not choose the founder's identity. Avoid empty traits such as “human”, “premium”, or “innovative” unless they are translated into observable choices.

### 04. Brand Values

Fields:

- **Core values:** three values.
- **What each value means and does not mean:** one paired rule for each value.

For each value record observable business behavior, customer implication, trade-off, violation, and whether it is evidenced practice or founder commitment. Values must help the business decide what to do, refuse, prioritize, or sacrifice. Do not infer values from customer preferences.

### 05. Conceptual Territory

Fields:

- **Central concept**
- **Key ideas:** three concepts supporting the central idea.
- **Metaphors:** one or two natural metaphors or images.
- **Cultural references:** two references that clarify the territory without inviting imitation.

Develop the territory from approved positioning, audience meaning, founder truth, existing history, and category patterns. Explain why each metaphor or reference fits. Record cultural risks, category confusion, stereotypes, invented heritage, and what must not be imitated.

Treat this as an approved conceptual direction for the later visual phase, not as permission to choose colors, typography, logos, composition, photography, illustration, or motion.

### 06. Voice & Tone

Fields:

- **Voice characteristics:** three observable characteristics.
- **Tone by situation:** everyday, high-stakes, and intimate situations, adapted when the business needs different contexts.
- **Expressions to use:** two aligned phrases or structures.
- **Expressions to avoid:** two words, phrases, or structures.

Use audience language and any real founder writing samples. Define directness, sentence rhythm, vocabulary, technical depth, formality, warmth, humor, metaphor, uncertainty, evidence, and calls to action through observable rules.

Cover relevant situations such as first contact, education, sales, uncertainty, error, payment, support, rejection, and success. Include short do/don't examples in the website language and record accessibility, reading-level, and localization constraints.

### 07. Messaging

Fields:

- **Core message**
- **Key claims:** three claims within the approved proof limits.
- **Narrative pillars:** three recurring story themes.

Translate approved positioning and offer value into a small repeatable message system. For each material claim record customer relevance, proof, qualification, prohibited stronger interpretation, and useful context.

Messaging guides later copy; it is not finished website copy. Narrative pillars organize stories and cannot create unsupported claims.

### 08. Distinctive Codes

Fields:

- **Conceptual codes**
- **Recurring ideas:** two ideas that give the brand a recognizable rhythm.

Include verbal patterns, phrases, symbols, behaviors, structural devices, or other recognizable assets only when relevant. Classify each as:

- **Existing and evidenced** — already used consistently or recognized.
- **Candidate** — strategically promising but not yet recognized.
- **Prohibited** — generic, copied, misleading, inaccessible, or strategically wrong.

A workshop-generated phrase, symbol, or behavior is a candidate. Recognition is a future measurement question.

### 09. Boundaries

Fields:

- **What the brand should never communicate**
- **Concepts to avoid:** two ideas or themes that do not belong.
- **Perceptions to avoid**

Cover audience fit, category meaning, claim limits, ethics, culture, language, customer experience, tone, and competitor codes. Boundaries should protect focus and trust without arbitrarily blocking visual exploration.

### 10. Creative Principles

Fields:

- **Principles for campaigns:** two rules.
- **Principles for content:** two rules.
- **Principles for storytelling:** two rules.

Write a small set of observable decision rules. Each rule should help two people judge whether an execution fits. Connect the rules to the approved essence, personality, messaging, comprehension needs, and boundaries. Do not turn them into visual specifications.

## Human decision protocol

First prefill every field that can be imported or responsibly recommended from approved sources. Then identify only the unresolved decisions that materially affect the concept.

Ask one missing question per turn. Prefer a specific decision question over asking the participant to write a whole block. When an abstract choice is difficult, offer two or three evidence-grounded directions and explain the trade-off. Accept `unknown` and keep it as a hypothesis rather than forcing a polished invention.

Research may test relevance, credibility, and risk. It cannot invent purpose, values, founder history, or identity boundaries.

## Approval and output standard

Before saving, use the checkpoint limit in `CLAUDE.md`: at most five bullets and one confirmation question. Show the decisions that materially define the concept, plus the largest unresolved contradiction or risk. Keep the full detail in the file.

The final `06-brand-concept.md` must include:

1. scope, source files, website language, approvals, and unresolved conflicts;
2. all ten blocks and every field in the schema above;
3. an evidence label and source pointer for each material decision;
4. a claim-to-proof and qualification table;
5. voice rules with do/don't examples;
6. existing, candidate, and prohibited distinctive codes;
7. hypotheses and decisions still requiring testing;
8. a `D1`–`D10` Website message handoff using the stable outcome contract in `SESSION-AND-EVIDENCE.md`, preserving the complete business proposition and adding observable brand expression;
9. a concise Visual Direction handoff describing what is locked, open, and risky.

The result is complete when the Visual Direction tool and the later `/structure` and `/copy` (or `/express-copy`) can use it without asking the participant to recreate approved strategy.

The Website message handoff is not a slogan summary. It must retain the concrete audience situation, alternative friction, offer, mechanism, meaningful difference, price or commitment, proof state, fit, objections, and action. Brand purpose and concise voice may sharpen these decisions but may not replace or remove them.

## Visual Direction boundary

The next Visual Direction phase owns `visual-direction.md`: Visual Foundation, Name & Typography, Color System, Composition, Page Architecture, Illustration & Materiality, Graphic Language, Distinctive Visual Codes, Visual Restrictions, and AI Visual Direction. Page Architecture supplies visual archetypes, focal rules, alternation, density, and a possible draft sequence. It does not finalize the business-led page list, section order, message sequence, proof placement, content requirements, or CTA path. The export may restrict photography but does not establish a positive photography direction unless it says so explicitly.

Use `06-brand-concept.md` and the approved research and offer files as its inputs. Then `/structure` or `/express-copy` reads the export and makes the final content-architecture decision. The visual system must express the approved business context without overriding evidence, claim limits, comprehension, conversion logic, accessibility, or commercially necessary content.

## Conflict protocol

- Market, audience, offer, or positioning conflict → identify the upstream source and do not overwrite it silently.
- Purpose, values, history, or identity-boundary conflict → the founder decides.
- Conceptual expression conflict → propose options grounded in the approved inputs and ask for one decision.
- Claim or proof conflict → the evidence limit wins.
- Visual expression conflict → resolve it during Visual Direction within the approved concept and website constraints.

Never average incompatible ideas into generic language.
