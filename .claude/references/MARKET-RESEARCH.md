# Market and Competitor Research Method

## Research question

Research exists to support a website decision. Define the question before browsing, for example:

- Which alternatives does this audience use in the triggering situation?
- Which promises are crowded, and which needs appear underserved?
- What proof and risk-reversal patterns appear credible in this category?
- Which words do customers use for the problem and desired outcome?

## Alternative set

Include three types:

1. **Direct:** similar offer for a similar customer and outcome.
2. **Indirect:** a different type of solution for the same progress.
3. **Status quo:** doing nothing, manual work, internal help, generic tools, free content, or an existing habit.

Do not accept the participant's named competitors as the complete market.

## Source priority

Prefer:

1. official offer, pricing, documentation, and product pages;
2. current customer reviews and public discussions with enough context;
3. trade bodies, regulators, public datasets, and reputable research;
4. credible journalism or expert analysis;
5. aggregators only as discovery aids.

Search-result snippets are leads, not evidence. Open the page.

## Minimum useful workshop pass

Within the available time, aim for:

- 3–5 relevant direct alternatives;
- 2–4 indirect or status-quo alternatives;
- at least 2 independent sources of customer language;
- an offer and message comparison;
- contradictions and missing information;
- 3–5 implications for the website.

These are guidance for coverage, not a quota. Stop early if candidates are irrelevant; expand if the market structure remains unclear.

## Decision strength

Classify every conclusion that will shape the offer or website:

- **Evidence-backed:** supported by relevant customer behavior, direct customer research, transaction or usage data, or several independent sources that agree.
- **Directional:** supported by a limited competitor/review sample but not direct validation with the priority audience.
- **Founder hypothesis:** based mainly on participant belief or one anecdote.
- **Unknown:** the search did not produce usable evidence.

Participant approval selects a direction; it does not upgrade the evidence strength. Never rename a founder hypothesis “validated” or “confirmed audience insight” merely because the participant likes it.

For every material conclusion state both:

1. **Safe to claim now** — what the website may state as fact.
2. **Safe to test** — what may appear only as a clearly framed proposition in a measurable experiment.

If no independent customer evidence is found, say that the audience insight remains unvalidated and specify the next direct research needed.

## Competitor comparison

Capture only what affects customer choice:

| Alternative | Best-fit customer or situation | Core promise | Mechanism or offer | Price/commitment | Proof | Risk/friction | Message pattern | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not award arbitrary numeric scores. Explain meaningful differences in words.

Capture operational and commercial signals when they affect the proposed offer:

- whether delivery, setup, onboarding, support, taxes, or materials are included in price;
- price range and the meaningful reasons prices differ;
- fulfillment model, timing, geography, capacity, and dependencies;
- trust mechanisms and failure complaints;
- evidence that the business could enter at a materially lower price or stronger promise.

When a proposed price sits materially outside the observed range, flag the discrepancy for the offer stage. Do not treat “cheaper” as differentiation until cost and delivery feasibility are understood.

## Differentiation test

A useful differentiation idea must pass all four checks:

- **Relevant:** matters to the priority audience in the chosen situation.
- **Distinct:** important alternatives cannot credibly claim the same thing in the same way.
- **Provable:** the business has or can create believable evidence.
- **Deliverable:** the offer can consistently provide it.

If it fails, save it as a hypothesis or aspiration.

Absence from the reviewed sample is not proof that no competitor offers something. Write “not found among the alternatives reviewed” and state the search limit.

## Research integrity

- Record market and geography.
- Note source and access date.
- Separate current facts from historical examples.
- Do not infer market size from a few posts.
- Do not infer purchase intent from engagement.
- Do not call a competitor weak because its copy is different.
- Do not copy protected language or design.
- Record meaningful disconfirming evidence.
